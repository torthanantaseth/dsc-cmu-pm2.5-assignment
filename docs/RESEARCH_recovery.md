# Recovery, relief and compensation — verified source pack

Research compiled 4 September 2026. Every figure carries a source URL and the
source's date. Items marked ⚠️ could not be verified — check before citing.

> Purpose: to let Part D of the report propose **costed** measures rather than
> sentiments. A recommendation such as "the government should help people" earns
> nothing. A recommendation with a price tag, a named payer, a trigger condition
> and an honest statement of what the analysis does not support is the 20-mark
> answer.

## The one comparison to build the recommendation around

| | Baht |
|---|---|
| Chiang Mai's **annual PM2.5 welfare cost** (PIER, 2019 valuation) | **70,356,000,000** |
| Equipping all ~666,000 occupied Chiang Mai households with the CMU ฿3,600 clean-air-room kit | **2,398,000,000** |
| **As a share of one year's damage** | **3.4%** |

At the registration-based household count (921,573) it is ฿3,318 M, or 4.7%. At a
฿4,000 commercial purifier, ฿2,664 M / ฿3,686 M. Sources for every input below.

**The honest caveat that must accompany it:** the ฿70,356 M is a
willingness-to-pay welfare valuation, not avoided cash outlay, and the kit's
*effectiveness* — what indoor concentration it actually achieves — was never
published (§1.2). So this is an order-of-magnitude argument for why the measure is
worth costing properly, not a benefit–cost ratio. Say that.

### 1.2 DIY and low-cost options

**CMU "30-baht" fan + HEPA sheet.** Prof. Dr Khuanchai Supparatpinyo, Research

**~฿30**, lifespan ~4 weeks normally, ~2 weeks in severe episodes.

⚠️ **This is the single most important missing number in the whole cost model** —

## 5. Precedents for compensating populations during pollution episodes

### 5.1 Thailand — the mechanism that already exists, and the gap in it

**Disaster-zone declaration.** On **5 April 2569** the government declared Chiang
Mai, Lamphun and Phayao disaster zones for forest fire / PM2.5.
**Trigger: PM2.5 above 125 µg/m³ for 5 consecutive days.** This unlocks governors'
authority over **เงินทดรองราชการ** emergency advance funds.
[PRD](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/33/iid/491804)

**Payment schedule attached to that fund** (revised, effective 6 March 2569, DDPM):

| Item | New rate | Previous |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency living expenses (total loss of home) | ฿4,900/household | ฿3,800 |
| Livelihood tools / working capital | ฿13,500/household | ฿11,400 |
| Housing rent | ฿2,500/month, ≤2 months | ฿1,800 |
| Survival package | ฿1,000/household | ฿700 |
| Severe injury (≥3 days hospitalised) | ฿5,000 | ฿4,000 |
| Funeral | ฿35,700/person | ฿29,700 |
| House repair | ≤฿88,600 | ฿49,500 |

[queensirikit.prd.go.th, 2 Mar 2569](https://queensirikit.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/39/iid/481398)

> ⚠️ **This is the gap your recommendation can fill.** Every category is written for
> floods and storms — physical damage, injury, death. **None of them fits "I could
> not work, could not open my shop, and had to buy a purifier because the air was
> toxic for six weeks."** There is no per-household cash line for a pollution
> episode.
>
> The concrete proposal: add a **หมวดภัยจากมลพิษทางอากาศ** line to the same
> regulation, with a per-household seasonal payment and a per-SME payment, using
> the **existing 125 µg/m³ × 5 days trigger** and the **existing disbursement
> plumbing**. No new agency, no new law, no new payment system — an amendment to a
> schedule. That is concrete enough for someone to act on, which is exactly what
> Part D asks for.
>
> And your model connects to it: a next-day exceedance classifier could move the
> trigger from *retrospective* (five days already endured) to *anticipatory*.
> Be honest about what that costs — at your chosen operating point, some days
> would be declared that turn out fine.

**Why it has not been escalated to a national disaster** (Sonthi Kotchawat,
environmental academic): fear of deterring Songkran tourism, insurance and
aviation consequences, and the political cost of the bans that would follow.
[Policy Watch](https://policywatch.thaipbs.or.th/article/environment-180)
⚠️ Article date inconsistent in extraction; re-check.

**TDRI's six proposals** (4 Apr 2024): dedicated PM2.5 agency under the PM; carbon
tax/credit; a northern airshed sandbox; integrated big data; consolidated budget;
cross-border cooperation. TDRI found FY2567 PM2.5 budget was **89.2% concentrated
in DDPM alone**. [tdri.or.th](https://tdri.or.th/2024/04/pm2-5-solution-northern/)
⚠️ The extracted budget total "17,529.98 billion baht" is certainly a units error
(almost certainly *million*). Verify before citing.

**Why it transfers to Chiang Mai:** funded by a **pre-existing ring-fenced worker
welfare levy**, not general revenue; triggered by an **automatic air-quality
threshold** (GRAP stage), not discretion; limited to **registered** workers, which
caps cost and creates a registration incentive. Thailand's analogue: the Social
Security Fund Section 40 register for informal workers, or provincial labour
office registers.
**Honest weakness:** reporting notes registration is incomplete; coverage of
informal workers is the binding constraint, and no disbursement-rate evaluation
was found.

### 5.3 California — in-kind clean-air infrastructure

**AB 836 Wildfire Smoke Clean Air Centers** (CARB, statewide). Grants to schools,
libraries, community and senior centres to install filtration and operate as
**public clean-air shelters**. Funds up to **100% of eligible costs, including a
five-year supply of replacement filters.** Eligibility: multiple days of AQI ≥151
from smoke in the past 5 years + serves vulnerable populations + public access.
[CARB guidelines](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2022-01/CARB%20-%2001-21-2022%20-%20Wildfire%20Smoke%20Clean%20Air%20Center%20Grant%20Guidelines.pdf) ·
[Valley Air District](https://www.valleyair.org/grants/ab-836-clean-air-centers-pilot-program/)

**Bay Area AQMD Clean Air Filtration Program** — home units to low-income residents
with diagnosed respiratory conditions via health centres; filtration in **16
schools** (12 under a **US$2 M** CARB award in 2018).
[baaqmd.gov](https://www.baaqmd.gov/about-air-quality/wildfire-air-quality-response-program/clean-air-filtration-program)

> **The design difference worth arguing:** California funds **five years of
> replacement filters up front**. That is the recurring cost that kills Thai clean
> air rooms after year one — Thailand's 4,875 rooms have no published
> filter-replacement budget. A recommendation that says "fund the filters, not just
> the boxes" is specific, cheap, and nobody is doing it.

### 5.4 Beijing — a negative finding, state it as such

⚠️ **No household or business compensation mechanism found** in Beijing's air
pollution emergency response. The Heavy Air Pollution Contingency Plan
([english.beijing.gov.cn](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/latest/lawsandpolicies/202007/t20200723_1957677.html))
and the Three-Year Blue Sky Action Plan are **command-and-control only**: red-alert
school closures, odd-even driving, mandated industrial halts. Academic work instead
documents the *uncompensated* costs
([China Economic Review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1043951X2300069X)).
**Beijing is a useful counter-example — a mitigation-only model — not a
compensation precedent.**
